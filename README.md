## The entry

> On summer evenings my grandmother lifted the lid off the rice pot and the steam fogged her glasses, and I stood on a stool beside her, small enough to believe dinner would always smell like that and she would always be there to serve it.

237 characters, one sentence. The memory is grounded in concrete sensation — a summer evening, a grandmother's kitchen, steam off a rice pot fogging her glasses, a child standing on a stool — with a small honest detail (the fogged glasses) rather than a greeting-card sentiment. The ache sits in the last clause: a child small enough to believe dinner would always smell like that and she would always be there to serve it. The loss is never stated, only implied, which is what makes it linger after the sentence ends.
